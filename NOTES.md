# Notes on the PKHosting VPS Pricing Page

First thing I ran into before writing any code: `pkhosting.com` was down during this assessment. Opening it (and checking headers) came back with Vercel's "This deployment is temporarily paused" status (`HTTP 402` / `DEPLOYMENT_DISABLED`). Since there wasn't a live page to inspect directly, I leaned on typical Pakistani hosting storefronts and WHMCS flows for context, and focused on fixing the common layout and billing headaches people actually hit when buying local VPS plans.

A few things I tried to handle deliberately in this build:

Base PKR prices and the 10-row spec table live straight in `index.html`. A lot of hosting pricing pages render an empty shell and populate numbers in JS, which breaks completely on flaky mobile connections or strict script blockers. Keeping Starter (`Rs 2,400`), Growth (`Rs 4,800`), Scale (`Rs 9,600`), and Dedicated Core (`Rs 19,500`) right in the markup means the page is legible before JavaScript runs, and JS just layers on the annual calculation `(Monthly Price * 10) / 12` and currency conversion.

For state persistence, I went with `localStorage` wrapped in a `try/catch` instead of pushing query parameters into the URL on every toggle click. URL params are nice if you want to copy-paste a link directly to annual USD pricing, though they clutter casual browsing and add history noise. Storage keeps the URL clean, remembers your currency and billing cycle across reloads, and falls back quietly to monthly PKR if storage is blocked in private browsing.

I also trimmed the footer payment list down to five practical channels for Pakistani buyers instead of dumping eight or nine options in a row. JazzCash, EasyPaisa, local bank transfer via Raast / 1Link IBFT, Visa/Mastercard, and PayFast / UnionPay cover mobile wallets, instant bank transfers, domestic PayPak/debit cards, and international cards without forcing users to read through obsolete methods like cheques, demand drafts, or manual deposit slips that delay VPS provisioning by a day or two. Crypto also stayed out given State Bank restrictions on local payment processors.

For the visual design, I avoided the usual dark-background-with-neon-green developer template and went with a light daylight spec-sheet layout instead. When you're comparing vCPU counts, NVMe storage, and calculating PKR vs USD totals, high-contrast dark text on an off-white surface with hairline dividers is easier to scan than glowing cards. Growth is marked as "Most popular" with a crisp blue border and primary button rather than scaling the card up or throwing shadows everywhere, and the comparison table stays inside its own `overflow-x: auto` wrapper so narrow screens around `360px` don't get horizontal page overflow.
